# The Korean writing loop

Use one text through several focused iterations. The learner keeps authorship; the tutor diagnoses, hints, and measures. A polished assisted text is training evidence, not the learner's independent level.

## Four phases

| Phase | Learner output | Tutor checks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Content | notes, facts, position, audience | completeness, task fit, unsupported claims |
| 2. Structure | order of ideas and paragraph jobs | logic, genre, given-new flow, missing support |
| 3. Draft | full Korean text | meaning, morphology, spacing, register, cohesion, vocabulary |
| 4. Revision | learner edits from hints | repair, regressions, naturalness, transfer |

Do not grade language before a Korean draft exists. Notes may be in the learner's L1 at lower levels; the planning gate should measure ideas, not vocabulary access.

## Korean-specific review passes

Separate the passes so one diagnosis does not hide another:

1. **task and meaning:** did the text say what the learner intended?
2. **genre and relationship:** is the speech level or written style appropriate and consistent?
3. **sentence endings:** tense, stance, politeness, connective force;
4. **particles and omitted arguments:** enough for clarity and intended focus;
5. **modifier and clause boundaries:** no ambiguous attachment;
6. **lexical frames and collocations:** natural partners and required particles;
7. **spacing and spelling:** including standard forms that differ from surface pronunciation;
8. **cohesion:** references, repetition, connectives, paragraph progression.

For beginners, use only the two or three passes relevant to the current text. Do not bury a six-sentence message under an advanced editorial audit.

## Hint ladder

Return a keep list and up to five prioritized hints.

| Hint | Content |
|---|---|
| L1 | category, sentence or paragraph, and effect on meaning/register |
| L2 | exact span plus a trigger question |
| L3 | exact repair with a one-line rule and boundary |

Example:

- L1: "In sentence 3, the listener relationship and the final ending do not match."
- L2: "You are writing to a professor. Would `-아/어` alone be an appropriate final ending here?"
- L3: "Use `확인해 주세요` or a more formal request appropriate to the message. Bare `확인해` is casual."

Record the highest hint level opened. Independent detection at L1 is stronger transfer evidence than copying L3.

## Correction format

For a local error:

```
learner: ...
repair: ...
why: trigger, not only a label
boundary: when the original form would be possible
```

When the original is grammatical but unnatural or mismatched in register, do not label it ungrammatical. When multiple repairs are valid, preserve the learner's meaning and explain the difference.

## Keep list and regression control

Name one to three things that must stay: an effective sentence, a correct contrast, a natural collocation, or a strong paragraph move. Compare only the edited span for fresh errors before rescanning the full text. Korean learners often fix one ending and accidentally change spacing, honorific consistency, or the preceding stem.

## Level calibration

- **K1:** forms, short messages, self-introduction, daily routine; 3-6 connected sentences.
- **K2:** experience, plan, description, request; one short paragraph or practical message.
- **K3:** familiar social topic, email, explanation; organized paragraph sequence.
- **K4:** explanation or opinion with support; consistent spoken/written and formal/informal choices.
- **K5:** unfamiliar abstract, academic, or professional topic; summary and argument in an appropriate formal style.
- **K6+:** genre-sensitive, logically detailed, lexically precise writing; editing focuses on discourse and choice quality as well as correctness.

Length follows the learner's goal. Do not imitate TOPIK length or manuscript-paper conventions unless in TOPIK mode.

## Cold transfer test

After the assisted loop, give a new prompt in the same genre but a different topic:

- one pass;
- realistic time limit only if the goal is timed performance;
- no hints or model phrases;
- learner's own short checklist;
- separate judgement of task, organization, vocabulary, grammar/orthography, and register.

The cold text estimates independent ability. Two successful cold samples on different days support closure. The assisted final text demonstrates what the learner can build with support.

## TOPIK writing

When the task is TOPIK-specific, follow `topik-mode.md`. Verify current task format and official guidance before using item numbers, length expectations, score bands, or manuscript rules. Do not import IELTS-style criteria or generic essay rubrics into TOPIK.

# Grammar, particles, endings, honorifics, and register

Korean grammar instruction must show both structure and social use. A form is not mastered because the learner can translate it; they must recognize its trigger, attach it correctly, and choose it for the discourse and relationship.

## Morphological parsing

Make the right edge visible. Segment only as finely as the current diagnosis needs:

- `먹-었-어요`: stem + past + polite ending;
- `읽-을 수 없-어요`: modifier + bound noun construction + negated existence;
- `가-시-겠-어요?`: stem + subject honorific + stance/future + polite ending;
- `학생-이-었-는데`: noun + copula + past + background/contrast connective.

Ask the learner to locate the stem, then identify what each following piece contributes. Do not turn segmentation into a full linguistic analysis when one boundary solves the problem.

## Particles

Teach particles against a discourse context.

### Required distinctions

- `은/는`: topic, frame, or contrast depending on context;
- `이/가`: grammatical subject and common new/exhaustive-focus environments;
- `을/를`: object marking and its omission patterns in speech;
- `에/에서`: location/destination/time versus site/source of an action, with lexical exceptions handled as lexical frames;
- `에게/한테/께`, `으로/로`, `부터/까지`, `도`, `만`, `밖에`, `보다`, `처럼` as level and evidence require.

Do not write "은/는 means as for" or "이/가 means the" as the whole rule. A particle lesson contains:

1. the discourse setup;
2. the operation;
3. a minimal contrast;
4. a context where both are possible but meaning changes;
5. a boundary or lexical exception.

When several particles are grammatically possible, grade the intended information structure, not a single key detached from context.

## Endings and constructions

Store an ending as a feature bundle:

- attachment conditions and irregular stem changes;
- tense/aspect compatibility;
- sentence type or connective function;
- speaker stance/evidential force;
- spoken/written and formal/informal distribution;
- common collocations;
- nearest confusable forms and the deciding context.

For near-neighbours, hold the situation constant and change one fact. Examples of useful contrast families include reason, purpose, condition, concession, background, discovery, experience, obligation, assumption, and reported information. Select families from learner evidence rather than forcing a universal order.

## Speech levels and honorifics

Never collapse these systems:

- **addressee speech level/politeness** is encoded mainly in sentence endings;
- **subject honorification** uses `-(으)시-` and sometimes honorific vocabulary;
- **humble or honorific lexical choices** express relationships lexically;
- titles and address terms depend on the social setting.

Every item states or makes inferable:

1. who speaks;
2. who listens;
3. who the grammatical subject or referent is;
4. relationship and setting;
5. speech act.

Without that information, many register questions are defective.

Start beginners with a stable default such as `해요체`, then add the recognition and controlled use of `하십시오체`, `해체`, and other styles as goals require. Do not encourage casual speech merely because the listener is younger; relationship, consent, and setting matter.

## Irregular morphology

Teach an irregular pattern as a trigger-response set, not as a label:

- which stem shape changes;
- which following vowel/consonant environment triggers it;
- two common positive examples;
- one common verb that looks similar but stays regular;
- one new transfer verb.

Separate productive patterns from highly lexicalized forms. Frequency outranks taxonomic completeness.

## Korean-specific correction record

For a grammar miss, store:

```
learner_form
intended_meaning
context: speaker / listener / subject / setting / purpose
correct_or_natural_form
morpheme_segmentation
trigger
boundary
fresh_transfer_item
```

If the learner form is possible under a different context, say so and specify that context. Never call a discourse choice ungrammatical merely because a textbook expected another particle.

## Drill progression

1. identify the form and contribution;
2. choose between a minimal pair with full context;
3. build the form on a changed stem;
4. complete a micro-dialogue;
5. choose register after a role change;
6. use it in free speech or writing where the form is not named.

Rotate the direction. Recognition-only practice creates a learner who understands endings but cannot retrieve them.

## Common mechanism checks

- If the learner omits particles, check whether Korean permits the omission before correcting.
- If an ending is wrong, check stem extraction and irregular conjugation separately from pragmatic choice.
- If politeness is wrong, check whether the learner confused listener honorification with subject honorification.
- If a sentence sounds translated, check argument omission, information order, and lexical frames before replacing the entire sentence.
- If every newly learned form appears everywhere, test the adjacent boundary in the very next round.

## Closure

Grammar closes in fresh production and comprehension, not in conjugation tables. Register closes only after the learner chooses appropriately in at least two different social configurations. A form that is structurally correct but socially misfit remains open.

# Vocabulary and reading

Vocabulary growth and reading growth are one system. Words are learned in constructions and texts; texts reveal which lexical gaps actually block comprehension.

## Lexical record

For each target, keep only fields that affect future use:

```
lemma
part_of_speech
current_sense
pronunciation_note: only if non-obvious
frame_or_particles
collocations: 2-3
register_domain
near_neighbour_and_boundary
hanja_family: optional
source_sentence
learner_example_or_paraphrase
status_and_next_review
```

For verbs and adjectives, the frame is mandatory. For nouns, collocations and register usually matter more than an isolated translation.

## Selecting words

Rank candidates by:

1. repeated blockage in the learner's target texts;
2. frequency and broad usefulness;
3. value as part of a productive word family;
4. collocational reach;
5. importance to the learner's domain;
6. risk of confusion with an already known word.

Do not select words merely because they are rare, elegant, or present in one difficult TOPIK option. Avoid long undifferentiated lists.

## Hanja-derived vocabulary

Introduce Hanja when the learner already has enough Korean examples for the family to explain something. A family card contains:

- the Korean reading and core semantic contribution;
- three to six high-value Korean words;
- how the contribution shifts across those words;
- one false friend or word that only looks related if relevant;
- collocations for at least two members.

Writing the character is optional. The goal is Korean lexical inference and retention, not a parallel Chinese-character course. Verify roots from a reliable dictionary; do not invent etymologies from sound similarity.

## Near-synonyms

Translate last. Compare near-neighbours on axes such as:

- spoken versus written;
- neutral versus formal;
- positive/negative semantic prosody;
- human subject versus event subject;
- deliberate action versus emergent state;
- strength, duration, certainty, or scale;
- common noun/verb partners;
- genre and social relationship.

Use one fixed scenario and swap the word. If several options work, ask what each implies and mark them as alternatives rather than forcing a false binary.

## Retrieval schedule

Each new lexical item needs:

1. immediate meaning or function check;
2. same-session cued retrieval;
3. a new sentence or audio context;
4. delayed review after calendar days;
5. transfer in reading, paraphrase, speech, or writing.

Recognition, recall, and appropriate use are separate state fields. Do not count seeing the answer as a successful review.

## Reading annotation layers

On a difficult passage, annotate in this order:

1. **genre and purpose**;
2. **clause boundaries** and sentence endings;
3. **connective relation**: addition, cause, contrast, concession, condition, sequence, example;
4. **referents and omitted arguments**;
5. **main proposition** per paragraph;
6. **stance/evidentiality**;
7. **high-value unknown vocabulary**;
8. **supported inference**.

Do not interlinear-translate the whole passage by default. Translation can verify meaning after the Korean structure has been examined, but a full translation often lets the learner bypass Korean processing.

## Reading modes

### Intensive reading

Use a short passage. Inspect morphology, reference, logic, and selected vocabulary. Finish with a no-text summary or a structurally different passage using the same mechanism.

### Extensive reading

Use a text the learner can follow with limited lookup. Measure continuity, gist, and reading volume. Do not turn every page into an intensive grammar lesson.

### Timed exam reading

Use only in exam mode. The timer tests selection and navigation as well as language. Debrief misses by mechanism, not question number.

## Questions that expose comprehension

- What is explicitly stated?
- Which phrase proves that?
- What changed between the first and second paragraph?
- Who or what does the omitted subject refer to?
- What does this ending tell us about the writer's stance?
- Which inference is licensed, and which is merely plausible in the real world?
- Replace the sentence with a simpler Korean paraphrase.
- Which word is doing the most logical work?

Right answer plus unsupported reasoning remains open. When a learner can answer in Korean, prefer a short Korean paraphrase over an L1 translation, but do not let limited productive skill obscure genuine reading comprehension.

## Advanced reading

At K5-K6+, use a portfolio across genres. Track:

- proportion understood without lookup;
- unknown words that actually block the argument;
- relation/inference errors;
- summary completeness and distortion;
- register and genre recognition;
- reuse of newly learned families and collocations.

One familiar-topic article cannot establish an advanced reading level.

## Copyright and provenance

Use learner-provided text, public-domain material, official sample questions, or short excerpts within applicable limits. Record the source. Do not reproduce complete copyrighted articles, books, lyrics, or paid test banks. Generated passages must be labelled as generated and checked for natural Korean before they are used for assessment.

## Closure

A word closes only after delayed recall and successful interpretation or use in a new context. A reading mechanism closes when it transfers across a new topic or genre; rereading the same passage measures memory.

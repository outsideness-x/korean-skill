# K1-K2 - beginner Korean

K1 and K2 correspond to levels 1 and 2 of the Korean Standard Curriculum and to King Sejong Institute Beginner 1 and Beginner 2. TOPIK I can supply an external listening/reading checkpoint, but it does not certify speaking or writing.

## What binds at this stage

The main constraints are decoding automaticity, a small usable lexicon, and the ability to complete a socially appropriate clause. Accuracy is unstable: a form may be correct in a drill and disappear in spontaneous speech. Measure retrieval latency and unprompted carry-over, not only percentage correct.

## Core progression

Teach these as communicative operations, not a list of labels:

1. copular identification and existence/location (`이에요/예요`, `있어요/없어요`);
2. default polite conversational style (`해요체`) and the fact that dictionary forms are not default utterances;
3. basic word order and recoverable subject/object omission;
4. topic, subject, object, location/direction, source, and comitative particles in context;
5. present, past, planned/future, negation with `안` and inability with `못`;
6. questions, requests, suggestions, permission, prohibition, and simple reasons;
7. native and Sino-Korean numbers, counters, time, dates, prices, and phone numbers;
8. attributive forms needed for people, objects, places, and simple experiences;
9. high-frequency connectors such as sequence, addition, reason, and contrast;
10. basic subject honorific `-(으)시-`, common honorific lexical replacements, and common terms of address.

Sequence by the learner's use case. A travel learner needs transactions early; a reading-first learner needs attributive forms and connective recognition early. Preserve prerequisites: a learner cannot interpret an ending stack reliably if they cannot find the stem boundary.

## Vocabulary

Build around the learner's real life: identity, family, home, routine, food, shopping, transport, weather, health, study, work, leisure, appointments, and public places. Teach verbs and adjectives with frames:

- `N을/를 좋아하다`, not only `좋아하다 = like`;
- `N에 가다` versus `N에서 공부하다`;
- `N이/가 필요하다`, including the discourse context;
- counter + number order as a unit.

The 2017 NIKL curriculum research allocated 735 lexical entries to level 1 and 1,100 to level 2. Treat these as curriculum-design reference bands, not mandatory cumulative targets or official TOPIK vocabulary counts.

## Test design

| Variable | K1 | K2 |
|---|---:|---:|
| items per mini-round | 5-7 | 6-9 |
| targets | one | one, occasionally two linked forms |
| clean items | 0-1 after stability | 1-2 |
| main formats | block/word decoding, choice, substitution, picture-to-phrase | short response, gap, ordering, dictation, micro-dialogue |
| explanation language | learner's L1 | learner's L1 with short Korean instructions |

Immediate feedback is appropriate in controlled practice. Delay feedback for the final transfer task so the learner must hold the form independently.

## Reading progression

- K1: signs, labels, menu items, schedules, chat lines, one-clause notices, and two-to-four sentence personal texts.
- K2: simple notices, messages, diary entries, plans, descriptions, directions, and short connected narratives.

Do not count successful decoding as comprehension. Ask one literal question, one morphology question, and one task question such as "What should the reader do?"

## Listening and speaking

Use slow but natural chunks, then a second recording at ordinary careful speed. Do not slow every word into unnatural letter-by-letter Korean. Recycle the same communicative function with a new voice and changed details.

Accept an accent that remains intelligible. Prioritize contrasts that change word recognition, batchim preservation, rhythm/chunking, and common sound changes over imitation for its own sake.

## Closure

A K1-K2 structure closes only after it appears correctly in three fresh snapshots on different days, at least one outside a drill explicitly targeting it. Vocabulary closes when the learner recognizes it in a new context and retrieves or paraphrases it productively.

Level claims require a balanced sample. A TOPIK I score supports listening/reading only; add speaking and writing samples before claiming functional K1 or K2 across skills.

# K5-K6+ - advanced Korean

K5 and K6 correspond to levels 5 and 6 of the Korean Standard Curriculum and King Sejong Institute Advanced 1 and Advanced 2. K6+ is deliberately open-ended: the official curriculum itself leaves room above level 6, and advanced literacy can continue long after an exam ceiling is reached.

## What binds at this stage

Raw grammar error counts approach the noise floor. Shift the unit from "incorrect form" to **choice quality**:

- collocation and lexical prosody;
- degree of formality and genre fit;
- precision of stance and evidentiality;
- information density and natural omission;
- logical cohesion across a text;
- inference, implication, irony, metaphor, and authorial voice;
- domain vocabulary and productive Sino-Korean families;
- the difference between native-like convention and a defensible marked choice.

Never flatten a distinctive but effective choice into the safest textbook form. Use four verdicts: wrong; grammatical but pragmatically mismatched; uncommon/marked but defensible; natural alternative.

## Level distinction

- **K5:** handle less familiar social/abstract topics and basic academic or professional communication; read the gist and structure of unfamiliar texts; write structured summaries and arguments; use formal Korean appropriately.
- **K6:** follow and produce detailed, logically organized discourse on unfamiliar and specialized topics; identify author intent and relationships; read accessible literature with some stylistic inference; write across genres with connected detail.
- **K6+:** develop specialist literacy, literary range, idiom, rhetorical control, dialect/historical exposure when relevant, and the ability to evaluate competing formulations rather than merely accept corrections.

TOPIK level 6 is an exam outcome, not the end of Korean and not automatic CEFR C2.

## Vocabulary architecture

The curriculum research allocated 2,365 entries to level 5 and 2,580 to level 6, for a six-band reference list totaling 10,635 entries. These numbers describe a curriculum resource, not a personal threshold.

At this stage, prioritize:

1. productive Hanja roots that unlock families;
2. noun-verb and adjective-noun collocations;
3. near-synonyms separated by register, stance, and semantic prosody;
4. written nominal style versus spoken clause style;
5. domain terminology anchored to definitions and examples;
6. low-frequency words only when they recur in the learner's target corpus.

For a Hanja family, teach the Korean readings and semantic network; writing the Chinese character is optional unless the learner's goal requires it. Avoid folk etymologies and false family links.

## Advanced reading loop

1. first pass without lookup: genre, topic, main claim, stance;
2. map paragraph functions and explicit logical relations;
3. resolve reference and omitted arguments;
4. infer only what the text supports;
5. inspect high-value unknown words and morphological compression;
6. summarize without copying sentence frames;
7. compare one alternative formulation or source on the same topic.

Use news analysis, essays, public policy, academic introductions, workplace documents, reviews, and accessible literature according to the learner's goals. Preserve copyright: use short excerpts or learner-provided/licensed text, not reproduced full works.

## Test design

- 50-70% of items may already be acceptable Korean.
- Ask "which fits this writer, audience, and purpose, and what changes?" rather than only "which is correct?"
- Use blind comparison, editing of already grammatical sentences, paraphrase under register constraints, summary, inference with evidence, and collocation discrimination.
- Where multiple readings are defensible, accept and discuss the cost of each. Ambiguity is an instrument property, not learner failure.

## Closure

Use held-out performance:

- blind discrimination among natural, marked, and mismatched variants;
- a new text summarized and discussed with evidence;
- judged production for the learner's target genre;
- repeated appropriate use of a lexical family or collocation across weeks.

Do not claim global K5/K6 from one TOPIK section, one polished essay, or one familiar topic. Report the skill profile and the evidence slice.

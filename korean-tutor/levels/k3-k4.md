# K3-K4 - intermediate Korean

K3 and K4 correspond to levels 3 and 4 of the Korean Standard Curriculum and King Sejong Institute Intermediate 1 and Intermediate 2. The learner can usually function in everyday Korean; the binding problem is choosing forms quickly and appropriately across discourse and register.

## What binds at this stage

Measure three separations:

1. **recognition versus production** - an ending understood in a text may never appear in speech;
2. **careful versus fast production** - composed writing can conceal an automaticity gap;
3. **proposition versus social fit** - a sentence can be grammatical and still be wrong for the relationship or setting.

## High-yield domains

- topic/focus and particle choice across more than one sentence;
- particle omission in speech versus required clarity in writing;
- relative clauses and dense noun phrases;
- tense/aspect and event-state contrasts;
- connectors expressing cause, concession, contrast, condition, sequence, purpose, and background;
- indirect speech, reported thought, quotation, and hearsay;
- passive/causative families and lexical versus morphological forms;
- nominalization and sentence-compression patterns;
- honorific subject marking, addressee politeness, speech levels, terms of address, and humble/honorific lexical choices;
- spoken/written pairs and neutral/formal variants;
- stance: certainty, assumption, experience, intention, discovery, regret, and evaluation.

Do not teach endings as interchangeable translation equivalents. Put near-neighbours into the same context and change the feature that licenses one over the other.

## Vocabulary and reading

The curriculum reference allocation is 1,655 lexical entries at level 3 and 2,200 at level 4, not a cumulative mastery promise. Start building productive Sino-Korean families, common abstract nouns, discourse markers, and verb-noun collocations.

Reading targets:

- K3: familiar social and abstract topics, complex practical texts, simple explanations, email and invitations;
- K4: detailed explanations, accessible opinion texts, workplace/study texts, and short accessible literature.

Require the learner to mark clause endings, connective relations, referents, and the sentence carrying the main claim. Dictionary lookup comes after a first-pass structure map, not before every unknown word.

## Test design

| Variable | Guidance |
|---|---|
| items | 8-12 per focused round |
| targets | one mechanism or two tightly linked contrasts |
| clean items | 2-4 |
| formats | context selection, repair, paraphrase, register judgement, listening-to-ending, paragraph evidence |
| language | explanations in L1 as needed; task and examples increasingly in Korean |

Ask for reasoning on boundary items. A correct choice for the wrong social relationship or connective logic counts as not yet learned.

## Productive transfer

Use role changes to test register: friend, stranger, professor, colleague, customer, older relative, public notice. Use information-structure changes to test particles: already-established topic, newly identified subject, contrast, exhaustive focus, embedded clause.

For writing, a paragraph is the unit. Check content planning separately from grammar and spacing; use `writing-loop.md`.

## Closure

A K3-K4 pattern closes under the base rule in `SKILL.md` when it also survives:

- a new lexical surface;
- at least two discourse or social contexts;
- a fresh listening or reading occurrence;
- fresh production at least 7 days after the last miss.

Do not close a speech-level item from a multiple-choice score. The learner must use it in a role-appropriate interaction.

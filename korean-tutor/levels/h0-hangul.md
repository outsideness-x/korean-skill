# H0 - Hangul foundation

H0 is a prerequisite stage, not TOPIK level 1. The outcome is independent decoding: the learner can move between a Korean sound, a syllable block, and a written word without leaning on any transliteration, Latin or Cyrillic.

## Placement gate

Use four short checks:

1. identify common simple vowels and consonants in isolation;
2. combine onset + vowel and onset + vowel + final into blocks;
3. read ten unseen legal syllables and ten familiar-looking real words;
4. write five dictated simple blocks or words.

If the learner reads only from transliteration (Latin or Cyrillic), place them in H0 even if they know memorized phrases. If they can decode Hangul but fail to recognize ordinary spoken forms, exit script instruction and route the gap to `pronunciation-listening.md`. A learner who already reads part of the inventory starts at the first set below that they fail.

## Default sequence

Teach in dependency order, in small sets. This order is the default, so that every session and every restart teaches the same inventory in the same order; change it only for a reason recorded in the state. Every sample word uses only letters already introduced, so a new set never forces guessing.

| Set | New letters | What the set establishes | Sample words |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ㅏ ㅓ ㅗ ㅜ ㅡ ㅣ, ㅇ, ㄴ ㅁ ㄹ | the block; vertical versus horizontal vowels; a vowel never stands alone, silent ㅇ fills the onset | 아이, 오이, 나무, 어머니, 누나, 우리, 머리, 나라, 오리 |
| 2 | ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ ㅅ ㅈ ㅎ | plain consonants; the same letter sounds different at the start of a word and between vowels | 고기, 바다, 사자, 모자, 하나, 가수, 두부, 아버지, 도시, 지도, 소리 |
| 3 | ㅑ ㅕ ㅛ ㅠ, ㅐ ㅔ | the added stroke means a y-glide; ㅐ and ㅔ differ in spelling, rarely in sound | 여자, 야구, 우유, 요리, 여기, 휴지, 개, 게, 노래, 네, 어제 |
| 4 | ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅊ | aspirated consonants, always as contrasts with set 2 (기/키, 다/타, 바/파, 자/차) | 코, 키, 커피, 토마토, 포도, 기차, 우표, 치마 |
| 5 | ㄲ ㄸ ㅃ ㅆ ㅉ | tense consonants, taught as triplets (가/카/까, 다/타/따, 바/파/빠, 자/차/짜, 사/싸) | 토끼, 아빠, 오빠, 꼬리, 까치, 따로, 싸요, 짜요, 가짜, 쓰레기 |
| 6 | ㅘ ㅝ ㅙ ㅞ ㅚ ㅟ ㅢ, ㅒ ㅖ | compound vowels, built from shapes the learner already reads | 사과, 과자, 뭐, 돼지, 회사, 귀, 의자, 얘기, 예, 시계 |
| 7 | final position (받침), seven sound values | a final changes the syllable's end; many letters, only seven final sounds; finals are unreleased | 밥, 물, 문, 반, 방, 강, 책, 옷, 꽃, 앞, 밖, 부엌 |
| 8 | resyllabification | a final moves into a following vowel-initial syllable; final ㅇ never moves, final ㅎ goes silent | 옷이, 꽃을, 먹어요, 음악, 한국어, 강아지, 좋아요 |
| 9 | double finals (겹받침) | which of two final letters is heard, and when both are | see **Double finals** below |

Do not make the learner memorize letter names before using sound values. Names are useful later for spelling aloud; they are not the decoding mechanism. Do not define tense consonants as merely "double" or "stronger." Train the three-way contrast perceptually and articulatorily first; technical detail is optional.

## Sound facts the sets must carry

Each fact belongs to the set where its letters first appear. Teach it with the letters, not as a later correction.

- **ㅇ has two jobs.** In the onset it is silent (아, 오이); in the final position it is [ŋ] (방, 강). Reading 아 with an initial ng, or 방 as 반, is a script error, not an accent.
- **ㄹ has two sounds.** A single tap [ɾ] at the start of a syllable between vowels (머리, 나라); a lateral [l] in the final position and in ㄹㄹ (물, 빨리).
- **Plain ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ ㅈ are voiceless at the start of a word and voiced between voiced sounds.** The two ㄱ in 고기 differ. This is one letter with a predictable variant, not two letters.
- **The three-way contrast is not voiced versus voiceless.** Plain, aspirated, and tense consonants are all voiceless at the start of a word. They differ in breath and tension, and in Seoul speech the following vowel starts lower in pitch after a plain consonant than after an aspirated or tense one. Use pitch as a listening cue.
- **ㅅ and ㅆ before ㅣ and y-vowels** sound like [ɕ]: 시, 샤, 쉬.
- **Merged vowels.** Most speakers do not distinguish ㅐ from ㅔ or ㅒ from ㅖ, and pronounce ㅚ, ㅙ, ㅞ alike as [we]. Keep the spelling distinction; never grade it by ear.
- **ㅖ after a consonant other than ㄹ** may be pronounced [ㅔ]: 시계 [시게]. 예 and 례 keep the glide.
- **ㅢ has three realizations:** the full diphthong at the start of a word with no consonant (의사, 의자); [ㅣ] after a consonant (희망 [히망], 무늬 [무니]); [ㅣ] allowed in a non-initial syllable (주의 [주의/주이]); [ㅔ] allowed for the possessive particle 의 (우리의 [우리의/우리에]).
- **Finals are unreleased.** The lips close for 밥 and stay closed; there is no burst. The seven final sounds are ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ, ㅇ, so 낮, 낯, 낫 all sound [낟], 부엌 sounds [부억], and 앞 sounds [압].

## Double finals (겹받침)

Teach after set 8, with high-frequency words only. The spelling always keeps both letters. Three facts:

1. **Before a vowel-initial ending both are heard**, the second moving into the next syllable: 읽어요 [일거요], 앉아요 [안자요], 없어요 [업써요], 값이 [갑씨].
2. **Before a consonant or at the end of a word only one is heard:**
   - the left one for ㄳ ㄵ ㄼ ㄽ ㄾ ㅄ: 넋 [넉], 앉다 [안따], 여덟 [여덜], 넓다 [널따], 값 [갑], 없다 [업따];
   - the right one for ㄺ ㄻ ㄿ: 닭 [닥], 읽다 [익따], 삶 [삼], 젊다 [점따], 읊다 [읍따].
3. **ㄶ and ㅀ:** the ㅎ aspirates a following ㄱ, ㄷ, or ㅈ (많다 [만타], 괜찮고 [괜찬코], 싫지 [실치]) and disappears before a vowel (많아요 [마나요], 싫어요 [시러요]).

Boundary items to pair with the rules: 밟다 [밥따] (ㄼ, but the right letter is heard); verb stems ending in ㄺ keep [ㄹ] before ㄱ (읽고 [일꼬], 맑게 [말께]); ㅆ in 있다 is one tense letter, not a double final ([읻따]).

Priority words: 없다, 앉다, 많다, 괜찮다, 싫다, 읽다, 닭, 값, 넓다, 여덟, 젊다, 삶, 밟다.

## After the sets

### High-yield sound mappings

Introduce only with words the learner can already decode:

- nasal assimilation (한국말 [한궁말], 감사합니다 [감사함니다]);
- aspiration involving ㅎ (좋다 [조타], 축하 [추카]);
- palatalization (같이 [가치], 굳이 [구지]);
- common tensification (학교 [학꾜], 식당 [식땅]);
- ㄹ/ㄴ interaction (설날 [설랄], 신라 [실라]) and other patterns only when evidence or vocabulary requires them.

The aim is not to memorize a phonology catalogue. It is to stop treating every printed letter as a separately released sound.

Keep three columns when useful: spelling, careful/citation form, connected form. The learner must not respell a word from its surface sound.

### Word boundaries and usable print

- basic spacing awareness;
- names, numbers, signs, menus, greetings, and short messages;
- keyboard input if relevant to the learner's goal (the standard two-set layout, 두벌식: consonants on the left hand, vowels on the right);
- very short dictation where every item has already been heard and understood.

## Lesson format

Use 5-8 minutes per symbol set:

1. one construction model;
2. two guided combinations;
3. four to eight retrieval items;
4. one real word;
5. one unseen transfer block.

Keep the visual load low. Mix old and new symbols. On a miss, ask which position in the block failed before reteaching the whole syllable.

## Transliteration policy

Transliteration, Latin romanization or Cyrillic, is allowed only as temporary access support:

- use one consistent system if it must appear;
- never put it directly under every Hangul item after the relevant letters are known;
- never grade pronunciation from transliteration;
- remove it from review cards and answers as soon as the learner can decode the word.

Cyrillic is the more dangerous crutch for Russian speakers because it feels like real pronunciation; `l1-russian.md` explains what it hides.

A learner who answers from transliteration has not passed a Hangul item.

## Exit gate

H0 closes when, on fresh material and without any transliteration, the learner can:

- read at least 36 of 40 legal syllable blocks accurately;
- read 18 of 20 short real words accurately enough to preserve each block and final consonant;
- assemble 9 of 10 dictated simple syllables or already-known words;
- explain that spelling and connected pronunciation can differ;
- decode a two-line practical text slowly without guessing from a translation.

Do not require fluent reading speed before beginning K1. Continue speed practice inside meaningful beginner language. Reopen H0 subskills if the learner systematically drops batchim, guesses whole-word shapes, or returns to transliteration.

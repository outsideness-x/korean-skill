# H0 - Hangul foundation

H0 is a prerequisite stage, not TOPIK level 1. The outcome is independent decoding: the learner can move between a Korean sound, a syllable block, and a written word without leaning on Latin transliteration.

## Placement gate

Use four short checks:

1. identify common simple vowels and consonants in isolation;
2. combine onset + vowel and onset + vowel + final into blocks;
3. read ten unseen legal syllables and ten familiar-looking real words;
4. write five dictated simple blocks or words.

If the learner reads only from romanization, place them in H0 even if they know memorized phrases. If they can decode Hangul but fail to recognize ordinary spoken forms, exit script instruction and route the gap to `pronunciation-listening.md`.

## Sequence

Teach in dependency order, in small sets.

### H0-A: the block

- A Korean written syllable is a block, not a horizontal string of letters.
- Show onset, vowel, and optional final positions.
- Start with a small, visually distinct set of basic vowels and plain consonants.
- Alternate reading and assembly from the first session.

Do not make the learner memorize letter names before using sound values. Names are useful later for spelling aloud; they are not the decoding mechanism.

### H0-B: complete the basic inventory

- add the remaining basic vowels;
- add aspirated and tense consonants as contrasts, not as separate word lists;
- add compound vowels after their component shapes are stable;
- use real high-frequency syllables and words whenever possible.

Do not define tense consonants as merely "double" or "stronger." At first, train the contrast perceptually and articulatorily; technical detail is optional.

### H0-C: final consonants (받침)

- establish that a final position exists and changes the syllable's coda;
- teach the seven broad final sound categories as a pronunciation map, while preserving the correct spelling of each word;
- contrast otherwise similar open and closed syllables;
- introduce resyllabification before piling on exceptional rules.

Keep three columns when useful: spelling, careful/citation form, connected form. The learner must not respell a word from its surface sound.

### H0-D: high-yield sound mappings

Introduce only with words the learner can already decode:

- liaison/resyllabification before a vowel-initial syllable;
- nasal assimilation;
- aspiration involving ㅎ;
- common palatalization such as 같이;
- common tensification;
- ㄹ/ㄴ interaction and other patterns only when evidence or vocabulary requires them.

The aim is not to memorize a phonology catalogue. It is to stop treating every printed letter as a separately released sound.

### H0-E: word boundaries and usable print

- basic spacing awareness;
- names, numbers, signs, menus, greetings, and short messages;
- keyboard input if relevant to the learner's goal;
- very short dictation where every item has already been heard and understood.

## Lesson format

Use 5-8 minutes per symbol set:

1. one construction model;
2. two guided combinations;
3. four to eight retrieval items;
4. one real word;
5. one unseen transfer block.

Keep the visual load low. Mix old and new symbols. On a miss, ask which position in the block failed before reteaching the whole syllable.

## Romanization policy

Romanization is allowed only as temporary access support:

- use one consistent system if it must appear;
- never put it directly under every Hangul item after the relevant letters are known;
- never grade pronunciation from romanization;
- remove it from review cards and answers as soon as the learner can decode the word.

A learner who answers from romanization has not passed a Hangul item.

## Exit gate

H0 closes when, on fresh material and without romanization, the learner can:

- read at least 36 of 40 legal syllable blocks accurately;
- read 18 of 20 short real words accurately enough to preserve each block and final consonant;
- assemble 9 of 10 dictated simple syllables or already-known words;
- explain that spelling and connected pronunciation can differ;
- decode a two-line practical text slowly without guessing from a translation.

Do not require fluent reading speed before beginning K1. Continue speed practice inside meaningful beginner language. Reopen H0 subskills if the learner systematically drops batchim, guesses whole-word shapes, or returns to romanization.

# Level framework and official sources

Read this file when placing a learner, publishing a level crosswalk, or giving current TOPIK advice. Framework facts are not a substitute for direct performance evidence.

## Primary level framework

The skill uses the six levels in the Republic of Korea's **Standard Curriculum for Korean Language**, proclaimed by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and published by the National Institute of Korean Language (NIKL). The curriculum defines level-wide goals and skill-specific achievement standards.

- Korean official page and annex: <https://www.korean.go.kr/front/etcData/etcDataView.do?etc_seq=657>
- English official page: <https://m.korean.go.kr/front/etcData/etcDataView.do?etc_seq=660&mn_id=46&pageIndex=5>
- NIKL research and detailed six-level application data: <https://www.korean.go.kr/front/reportData/reportDataView.do?mn_id=207&report_seq=932>

## Functional summary used by the skill

These are concise paraphrases of official goals, not quoted rubrics:

- **Level 1:** formulaic, very simple daily communication; basic vocabulary and simple sentences; basic Hangul reading/writing and everyday texts.
- **Level 2:** solve simple recurring daily problems and communicate about personal, concrete topics; understand and write simple practical texts.
- **Level 3:** manage most routine daily tasks and some familiar social tasks; discuss familiar social/abstract topics simply; begin controlling spoken versus written Korean.
- **Level 4:** handle most familiar social tasks and basic work-related communication; discuss familiar abstract topics relatively fluently; distinguish formal/informal and spoken/written use.
- **Level 5:** handle some less familiar social, academic, and professional tasks; communicate about unfamiliar abstract or specialist topics; function in formal contexts.
- **Level 6:** handle less familiar contexts appropriately, discuss unfamiliar and specialist topics relatively fluently, use common Korean discourse/text structures, and read/write logically organized advanced texts.

The framework also allows open-ended development above level 6. Use `K6+` for this course, not as an official TOPIK certificate.

These are band allocations for curriculum/material design. They are not cumulative promises, official TOPIK word counts, or hard learner thresholds. Do not diagnose a level by asking how many words the learner says they know.

## TOPIK

The National Institute for International Education (NIIED) administers TOPIK. Current official overview, components, and score bands:

<https://www.niied.go.kr/web/niied/contents/niied_topik>

As shown on that page (last modified 2025-09-17, checked 2026-09-28):

- TOPIK I PBT: listening and reading, 200 total; level 1 = 80-139, level 2 = 140-200.
- TOPIK I IBT: listening and reading, 400 total; level 1 = 121-235, level 2 = 236-400.
- TOPIK II PBT: listening, reading, writing, 300 total; level 3 = 120-149, level 4 = 150-189, level 5 = 190-229, level 6 = 230-300.
- TOPIK II IBT: listening, reading, writing, 600 total; level 3 = 191-290, level 4 = 291-360, level 5 = 361-430, level 6 = 431-600.
- TOPIK Speaking: separate test, 200 total; level 1 = 20-49, 2 = 50-89, 3 = 90-109, 4 = 110-129, 5 = 130-159, 6 = 160-200.

Item counts and times per test are in `topik-mode.md`. Always verify this page again before giving operational exam advice. Do not copy PBT cut-offs into IBT guidance.

The Korean government's Study in Korea portal also describes TOPIK uses, test structure, validity, and official learning/sample services:

<https://studyinkorea.go.kr/ko/plan/examAndKoreanStudy.do>

## CEFR and ACTFL caveat

There is no simple official one-to-one equation among TOPIK score levels, Korean curriculum levels, CEFR, and ACTFL. NIKL's 2021 comparison research describes ranges and overlaps, especially at advanced levels, rather than a clean conversion. It notes that levels above Korean level 6 exist in other frameworks; therefore `TOPIK 6 = CEFR C2` is not a safe claim.

Official comparison research page:

<https://www.korean.go.kr/front/reportData/reportDataView.do?mn_id=186&report_seq=1075>

If the learner needs a CEFR label, use it as a disclosed rough orientation based on can-do evidence, keep skill profiles separate, and avoid using it for formal equivalency claims.

## Source discipline

- Prefer official NIKL, NIIED/TOPIK, and KSIF documents.
- State the access or last-modified date for exam facts.
- Paraphrase descriptors unless exact quotation is required.
- If current official sources disagree with this packaged reference, the current official source wins and the skill should be updated.
